A house with rooms

For a while the music had a page of its own and everything else lived in folders. Incipient Artistic Ventures is the answer to that: one house, with a room for each kind of work, sharing one mark and one rule.

The rooms are the listening room (Pure Audio), the living instrument (Infinite Jazz), the writing desk (Pages), the wall (Gallery), the studio (web design, built by hand), the store, the exchange for instruments, and this journal. Each one keeps its work legible: a title, a place, a path back to the source, and a reason to be here.

The rule is the same in every room: capture once, improve forever. Record the thing before it gets explained away, keep its source clean, and let it change without losing where it came from.

The door is unlisted for now. The house is being built with the lights on so it can be walked through before it is announced.

Mother Earth, from a live room to a release

Mother Earth started as a live performance in front of an audience. The recording was culled to the parts that held, taken apart, reworked, and finished back at the desk on the Mac. It is the first piece to leave the house as a release.

The open question is whether it leaves as it stands or gets one more pass. The version in the listening room is the one being judged. Either answer is fine; the point is that the choice is being made on purpose, and the earlier version stays on file whichever way it goes.

Infinite Jazz: a lineage, not a loop

The loops are no longer the product; the artifacts are. That sentence is the whole turn Infinite Jazz took this year. Improvisation that only generates more improvisation goes nowhere a listener can stand. Improvisation that leaves roots behind, each one finished and dated, with everything that grows from it attached, becomes an instrument that can be played for years.

Root 001 is the first such artifact. It is in preparation, and it will carry its own registry entry when it arrives: title, lineage, hash and date. How a lineage grows.

The evolution behind the house

Everything in these rooms is made inside a larger process: a way of working where a piece passes through several hands, each pass is bounded and written down, and nothing becomes final until Graham says so. The music, the scripts and this site were all built that way.

That process has its own name and its own public record, and this entry is the only place in the house that points to it. Walk through if you want to see how the work gets made; come back when you want to hear it.
