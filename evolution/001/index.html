Why keep a public development log?

A useful record makes it possible to see what changed and ask whether the change helped.

This is an edited public summary of the May 2026 Loop 001, revised October 24, 2026. The original was a design discussion developed with Claude. This summary presents its practical commitments; it makes no claim of independent model validation.

The question

How can a website explain a workflow involving several AI models without presenting a conversation as evidence that a finished platform exists?

The useful answer is a record of concrete work: a brief, a draft, a criticism, a revision, and a person’s decision about what to use.

The design decision

Use Evolution as a readable development log. Give each entry a clear status and a working link. Distinguish a proposed feature, an illustrative example, implemented software, and an evaluation result.

AI assistance can contribute to drafting and criticism. A person remains responsible for facts, attribution, and the decision to publish.

What remains to be built and tested

  • An inspectable end-to-end example with its actual inputs and outputs.
  • A fair comparison with a useful baseline.
  • Reliable context handoff and revision records.
  • A clear review step before personal or private material is published.

These are development goals. This note is a record of the approach, rather than evidence that those goals have all been delivered.

What belongs in public

Selected project decisions and examples can be useful to readers. Private conversations, unpublished work, personal circumstances, and third-party information require deliberate review before publication.

Public credit should describe the work each contributor actually did. A model’s confidence or agreement does not replace evidence.
