Phase Zero · Recorded run, 29 October 2026

What the first test showed.

A deterministic checker was tested offline on fictional five-lead reports, using a frozen fictional registry and supplied source records. Both the basic-check tier and the exact-source-check tier produced the report-level results below.

50

Synthetic reports: 25 clean and 25 with planted errors. No customer work or live web sources were tested.

25 / 25

Corrupted reports flagged by each tier. No corrupted report was accepted in this particular set.

25 / 25

Clean reports accepted by each tier. No clean report was rejected in this particular set.

The important limitation

The fictional registry contained enough information to flag every corrupted report, including the misquotation cases, without checking the quotations themselves. That makes this fixture easier than real research. These counts do not establish live accuracy, semantic reasoning, or the value of an AI judging panel.

Method, scoring, and remaining evidence gaps

Predictions were frozen before the separate answer key was opened. The recorded gate required at least 23 of 25 corrupted reports flagged, at least 23 of 25 clean reports accepted, and at least four of five reports flagged in each planted error family. Both tiers met those narrow synthetic gates.

The deeper tier compared exact quotations with supplied source text. It did not test an AI model, semantic paraphrases, independent judges, or live dispute resolution. There were zero external model or API calls.

This is a summary of the internal recorded run, not an independent reproduction. The checker and fixtures are not released on this page. No production accuracy or commercial cost claim follows from the result.

The next useful test is a new, independently labelled holdout with real dated sources, ambiguous or inaccessible evidence, and errors that a complete registry cannot reveal. False rejections and missed errors both need to be measured.

How a pilot works

We’re inviting up to five free design-partner pilots. Start with a description of one deliverable. We’ll agree the checks, reviewer, sharing arrangements, and timing before you send files. The purpose is to learn how well this approach handles real work.

  1. 1

    Agree a small scope

    A lead list of up to 25 entries, or a sourced report with up to 15 cited claims, plus the requirements it was meant to meet. Confirm what can be shared before sending it.

  2. 2

    Review the evidence

    A person uses tools to review the agreed entries or claims against the requirements and accessible sources. Findings distinguish supported, unsupported, and unverifiable claims.

  3. 3

    Agree a delivery date

    The target is three business days after scope and inputs are agreed; we’ll confirm the date with you. The report identifies evidence, source dates, and unresolved questions.

  4. 4

    Tell us what it was worth

    We ask for twenty minutes of honest feedback. Publication is optional: any anonymized example or case study needs your separate approval. Declining public use does not affect the free pilot.

Findings support your decision; they do not guarantee conversion, usefulness, or error-free work. Before files are shared, we’ll agree how tools may be used and a retention/deletion date covering the deliverable, review notes, and any saved source copies. Email correspondence also remains subject to the mail provider’s retention settings.

Checks agreed for the pilot

  • Conformance

    Format, count, required fields and deadline.

  • Existence

    Check links, citations, and available company records. Missing or inaccessible evidence is reported as a gap.

  • Source support

    Compare each in-scope claim with the source it cites; record contradictions and uncertainty.

  • Freshness

    Record source dates and check them against your agreed cutoff. A dated source does not guarantee that a fact is still current.

  • Duplicates

    Check for duplicates within the material provided. Prior resale or reuse cannot be ruled out without suitable comparison material.

Never on a report

“Valuable.” “Insightful.” “Will convert.”

Value shows up later, when buyers see what the work did for them. A report that promised value would eventually be wrong in public, so it sticks to what can be checked.

Where this is going In design

We’re exploring a future service in which buyers and providers agree how work will be checked before it is commissioned. The steps below are a proposed design. Today’s pilot provides findings; it does not settle payments or make binding dispute rulings.

  1. 1

    Agree the requirements

    Buyer and provider would set the checks and an acceptable difference between their scores. That tolerance is the proposed “splitting limit.”

  2. 2

    Record a self-check

    The provider would submit its assessment of each criterion alongside the work.

  3. 3

    Check the delivery

    A future service would combine basic checks with source review and flag anything that remained uncertain.

  4. 4

    Review disagreements

    Differences beyond the agreed tolerance could go to an independent reviewer, with fees and authority agreed in advance. This service is not offered yet.

  5. 5

    Build a review record

    With appropriate permission, findings could contribute to a provider’s record. Sharing that record across platforms remains a design goal.

How the pilot is reviewed.

  • Agree the checks first.

    Define the requirements, accessible evidence, reviewer, and treatment of uncertainty before the pilot begins.

  • Evidence first, with human review.

    A person reviews findings with tools. Model provenance is recorded where known; unknown provenance limits claims of independence. A cross-provider judging panel has not been validated.

  • Payment decisions stay with you.

    Pilots are free. The office does not hold funds, release payments, or make binding rulings about your vendor.

  • Test the checker, too.

    The recorded 50-case result is disclosed with its limitations. New holdout and live-source tests are planned; ongoing seeded monitoring and live catch rates are not established.

  • Record evidence and its limits.

    Findings identify the references consulted, access dates, and gaps. Any saved source copies, hashes, and retention period must be agreed in scope; a complete source archive is not promised.
