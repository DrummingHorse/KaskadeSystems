How a pilot works

Five design-partner pilots are open, and they're free. You send one real deliverable. You get back findings with the evidence, and the checks get tested against real work instead of synthetic cases.

  1. 1

    Send one deliverable

    A lead list of up to 25 entries, or a sourced report with up to 15 cited claims, plus the requirements it was meant to meet.

  2. 2

    Every line gets checked

    Each entry or claim is tested against your requirements and against its own sources. The checks are tool-assisted and reviewed by a person.

  3. 3

    Findings in three business days

    What holds up, what doesn't, the evidence for each, and anything that couldn't be verified, stated plainly.

  4. 4

    Tell us what it was worth

    Twenty minutes of honest feedback, and permission to use anonymized results. That's the whole price of a pilot.

Findings are evidence, not a guarantee. The office doesn't judge whether a lead will buy or a report will be useful. It checks what can be checked and says what couldn't be. Your files are deleted after the pilot unless you ask us to keep them.

What a findings report looks like

Every pilot ends in a findings report. It borrows the parts of a hallmark, which English silver has carried since 1300: who made the work, what standard it was held to, how it scored, who checked it, and when.

Where this is going In design

AI agents have started paying each other for work. The payment rails prove money moved; nothing yet proves the work was real. The Assay Office is designed to sit in that gap, using a rule metals traders already trust: both sides assay, small gaps are split, and big ones go to an umpire named in the contract.

  1. 1

    Stake the claim

    Buyer and provider lock the spec and a splitting limit before work starts.

  2. 2

    Deliver with a self-assay

    The provider submits the work with its own signed score for each criterion.

  3. 3

    Run the streak test

    Fast checks on every delivery: spec, links, registries and duplicates.

  4. 4

    Split or call the umpire

    Inside the limit, the job settles. Outside it, an umpire assay decides, paid for by the side further from its result.

  5. 5

    Stamp the hallmark

    The result joins the provider's record, which travels between marketplaces.

Streak test

The touchstone

Spec conformance, link checks, registry lookups and duplicate detection, on every delivery.

Passed a 50-case synthetic test; running in pilots

Fire assay

The cupel

Every claim read against the source it cites. Designed to use models from different labs, with a person on anything disputed.

Done by a person with AI help during pilots

Umpire assay

The referee

A second, independent assay when buyer and provider disagree beyond the splitting limit.

Designed, not offered yet

The assayer is never the miner

  • Standards come before the work.

    Criteria are agreed before checking starts, so nobody grades toward an answer.

  • A different hand judges.

    Lookups go first. Where a model helps, it comes from a different lab than the one that made the work.

  • The office never holds the gold.

    It never holds anyone's money. Payments stay between you and your vendor.

  • The office assays itself.

    Known-bad deliveries get seeded into the queue, and catch rates will be published once there's enough real work to count.

  • Evidence ships with every finding.

    Every cited page is archived at intake, and the report carries its hash.
