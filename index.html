Independent project · Early development

Several AI models.
One human-led workflow.

Kaskade explores a practical question: can a deliberate sequence of AI drafts, criticism, and revision make work more useful? A person sets the goal, checks the evidence, and decides what to keep.

Current stage: concept and workflow development. This website explains the approach and records design work. Its diagrams are illustrations; this page does not run AI models. The proposed modules below are development directions.

01  /  The question

Can a second perspective improve the work?

AI systems can produce useful drafts and still miss an assumption, repeat an error, or sound more certain than the evidence allows.

Kaskade explores an explicit handoff: pass the task, constraints, source material, and unresolved questions into the next stage, then inspect what changed.

Additional stages also add cost, delay, and opportunities to repeat mistakes. The useful question is whether a workflow improves a defined task enough to justify them.

01 / CONTEXT
Keep the brief intact.

Carry the goal, constraints, and source material between stages.

02 / REVIEW
Make criticism specific.

Ask what is unsupported, missing, or inconsistent, and record the revision.

03 / JUDGMENT
Keep a person in charge.

Model agreement is not verification. A person checks the sources and chooses the result.

Model names here illustrate possible role assignments. They do not imply a commercial partnership or that this site connects to those services. A useful workflow may need fewer stages.

01
Analyze
A wide-context model dismantles the prompt into its load-bearing assumptions, constraints, and unknowns. What is actually being asked.
02
Expand
A generative model widens the surface area. Multiple directions, deliberate divergence, no pre-commitment to a single answer.
03
Refine
A precision model rewrites each direction for clarity, structure, and signal. The craft pass. Tightens without collapsing.
04
Critique
An adversarial model attacks the refined drafts. What's weak, what's missing, what's derivative. The honest reader.
05
Synthesize
The master channel compresses the surviving material into one output. One voice. One artifact. Traceable back to every stage above.
Define a stopping rule, a time budget, and a quality check before starting. More passes do not guarantee a better answer.
03  /  A worked example

From a rough claim to a checkable update.

Constructed illustration. These sample edits explain the process; they are not a recorded model run or a performance comparison.

The sample brief: explain an early prototype clearly, preserve uncertainty, and invite useful feedback.

Draft: “Our tool transforms your notes into perfect project plans.”

Review: “Perfect” is unsupported. What does the prototype do today? What still needs checking?

Revision: “We are testing a prototype that organises project notes into a draft plan. It still needs human review for missing tasks and incorrect assumptions.”

Human check: compare the revision with the actual prototype and source notes before publishing.

The evidence needed next: compare this process with one model on the same task, using predefined quality criteria, elapsed time, cost, and failure cases.

05  /  Architekt

An image workflow with visible revisions.

Architekt is a proposed way to organise image work into explicit stages, from a brief and composition through rendering and review.

The goal is to retain prompts, model attribution, and the reason for each revision. The diagram shows the intended process; this website does not provide image generation or a revision service.

06  /  SKRAPR

Context you can choose and review.

SKRAPR is a proposed tool for turning selected notes and documents into a brief that can travel between stages. Import connectors and automated extraction are development goals; this site does not connect to or read your accounts.

The design requirements are explicit permission, visible source material, editable summaries, and a clear way to remove material. The quantities in the diagram are fictional examples.
